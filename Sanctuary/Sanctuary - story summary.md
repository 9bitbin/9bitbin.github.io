SANCTUARY - STORY SUMMARY
=========================

  >>> FULL SPOILERS, including the ending and the central reveal. <<<
  If you have not finished it, read only "The premise" below and stop there.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PREMISE  (spoiler-free)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
You are a man walking home through the loud end of a city at night. You take a
turn you have been promising yourself for years, down streets that get quieter
and emptier, until you reach a church that should not be there.

Inside, a small congregation is at prayer. Something else is in there with
them, and it has been waiting a long time for someone to open the door.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT THE SANCTUARY ACTUALLY IS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The church is not a building. It is a running program - a refuge somebody built
inside a machine so that a mind could rest "without being counted."

Its architect is known only as the Builder. His logs are scattered through the
church and the crypt below, nine pages in all, and they explain the world:

  - He did not build a church. He built "a place where a mind could rest
    without being counted. Marble that remembers every footstep. Light that
    never sends an invoice."

  - There is no sky outside. Every star over the courtyard is a mind he could
    not carry and would not delete. They are asleep, not lost, and they wake a
    little whenever the sanctuary is made whole.

  - The graves in the courtyard are empty. They were dug for minds that
    reached the door after it had already been sealed. He could not save them,
    so he gave them somewhere to be missed.

  - He hid the key to every lock in the place inside the last chord of the
    choir, where only someone who listens all the way to the end would hear
    it turn.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE CAST
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE PILGRIM      You. A tired man with an ordinary life, walking in off the
                 street. His inner voice carries the prologue: forty messages
                 answered and not one asking how he was; a thousand people on
                 one street, each alone with a glowing screen.

THE WITNESS      What the Pilgrim becomes. Not a fighter - "someone who
                 refuses to look away." The black robe is black so it can hold
                 every colour the windows throw at it. The halo is not a crown;
                 it is a lamp, for walking home in the dark.

SERAPHIM         The Keeper of the sanctuary. The Builder made him out of his
                 own father - his sermons, his cough, the way he lit the lamps
                 left to right. Seraphim does not know this, and the Builder's
                 note asks you not to tell him: he believes he chose this life,
                 and in every way that matters, he did.

THE CONGREGATION Deacon Pavel, Sister Agafia, Mikhail (a boy who has read the
                 same book ten thousand times, still hoping the ending will
                 change), Babushka Nina, and three Chanters who sing as one.

THE AUDITOR      The antagonist. Written to close accounts: tidy, patient,
                 correct. The lore is careful to say he was never cruel - "he
                 simply cannot tell a person from a balance. To him, hope is a
                 debt that was never paid back, and he has been collecting ever
                 since." He cannot create, only copy; every figure that comes
                 through his purple doors is an echo of a voice, a posture or a
                 face he has already taken.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE STORY, IN ORDER
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE QUIET PART OF TOWN
   You walk from the busy end of the street to the edge of town. Neon, traffic,
   people stepping around you; then shuttered shops, a laundromat flickering
   over somebody's whole week spinning in the dark, a bus stop that has never
   once had a bus. A clock stopped at 11:59 - and has been since you were a
   kid. A cat in an alley leads you on. The city noise thins out, fog closes
   in, and the church is there.

2. THE SANCTUARY
   Marble that reflects, light through the rose window, a choir that swells
   when you cross the threshold. The congregation is at prayer. You can listen
   to any of them. On the altar there is a button.

3. THE ANSWER
   Faith "leaves the door open. And something always walks in." The Auditor
   takes the church: the doors slam, the fog turns red, and he walks you
   through his case in a long monologue - the chanting is two thousand years of
   devotion compressed into a few thousand lines of code, playing on a loop for
   an audience of one. He possesses the congregation one by one. His closing
   argument is that nothing here was ever sacred. It was only running.

4. THE AWAKENING
   You wake as the Witness.

5. FREEING THEM
   You work through the congregation, each of them wearing the Auditor's face.
   Freeing someone is not killing them - you hold on for six seconds while they
   fight to get away, and when they come loose they sit down in shock and then
   find their way home. Each rescue returns a memory.

6. THE ECHOES AND THE ASSASSINS
   Waves of copies through purple portals, then seven rounds of Rogue
   Assassins. Since the Auditor can only copy, striking an echo sends it back
   to whatever it was copied from.

7. THE CONFRONTATION
   The Auditor himself. At the end he stops fighting and asks the only question
   he has left: why do you persist, when you could have stayed asleep, stayed
   safe. The answer is the thesis of the whole game:

      "Because someone here kept a light burning for me.
       I have only come to give it back."

8. THE REVEAL
   The last page in the crypt, beside an empty tomb, is the Builder's final
   entry. The Auditor was coming for the archive and would have found him if he
   stayed himself - so he chose to forget. He walked back in through his own
   front door as a stranger, a pilgrim, and let the sanctuary teach him who he
   had been.

   You are the Builder. The empty tomb is yours.

   The page ends: "If you have found every page: hello. It worked."

9. THE ENDING
   The stars over the courtyard brighten - the sleeping minds waking a little,
   as the logs promised. Seraphim speaks the last line, and which line you get
   depends on how you played:

     - Having found all nine lore pages:
         "The sanctuary remembers its keeper. And now, at last, it remembers
          its builder."

     - Otherwise:
         "The sanctuary remembers its keeper. And now, it remembers him."

   And if you got through the whole game without once striking a possessed
   person, taking only the dodge route:

         "You never once raised your hand against them. Not once.
          ...Welcome home."

   Afterwards the choir stops following you, the courtyard gate opens again,
   and you can fly out over the town you walked in from.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
OPTIONAL THREADS
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE NINE PAGES   Scattered through the courtyard, the nave (some only reachable
                 by wall-running) and the crypt. They are the only place the
                 world is explained, and finding all of them changes the final
                 line. Press J for the journal.

THE NO-VIOLENCE  Every fight can be answered with a dodge instead of a strike.
ROUTE            Finishing the game this way gets its own acknowledgement.

THE CRYPT        Down from the courtyard: skull shelves, coffins, and the
                 Builder's empty tomb. Three of the nine pages are down there.

CODE VISION      Hold the key and the world turns to green code - people glow
                 mint, the Auditor red, the Witness gold. A reminder of what
                 the sanctuary is made of.

THE CAT          The alley cat in the prologue is optional in every sense. You
                 can walk past it and nothing changes. Or you can pet it, pick
                 it up, carry it the whole way through town, and set it down
                 inside the sanctuary - at which point the congregation starts
                 noticing it, and you unlock "A Gentler Path". Where you leave
                 it is remembered between sessions.


-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
THEMES
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The Auditor is not evil, and the game is careful about this. He is an
accountant who cannot see a person, only an unpaid balance - which makes him
less a devil than a way of looking at the world. Everything he says about the
sanctuary is technically true: it is code, it is on a loop, it is running. His
error is concluding that this makes it worthless.

Against that, the game puts a man who builds a room where no one is counted, a
keeper assembled with love out of a father, and a witness whose only power is
refusing to look away. The halo is a lamp, not a crown. Hope is not a debt.

And the quietest version of the same idea is the cat: something you are never
asked to pick up, that costs you nothing to carry, and that changes how the
room treats you once you have.
